Once upon a time a kind girl lived near a quiet wood.
She packed bread and cheese and walked to the mill before sunrise.
The miller waved and gave her a small bag of warm grain.
A grey cat sat on the stone wall and watched the river.
The river sang over smooth rocks and carried leaves to the sea.
In the morning the farmer opened the barn and fed the horses.
The horses stamped and breathed white clouds into the cold air.
A boy named Tom found a red apple under the old tree.
He shared the apple with his sister and they both smiled.
The sister told a story about a fox who wanted to be kind.
The fox listened to the birds and learned to wait his turn.
Birds built a nest in the oak and sang until the sun was high.
A dog ran through the grass and brought back a stick.
The children laughed and threw the stick again and again.
Rain fell on the roof and made a soft drum in the kitchen.
Grandmother baked bread and the house smelled like yeast and fire.
The baker in town sold cakes on market day and counted coins.
A traveler asked for water and thanked the well with a bow.
Night came slowly and the moon rose over the hill.
Stars filled the dark like tiny lamps above the fields.
A shepherd counted sheep and hummed an old tune.
The sheep slept in a circle around a single lantern.
Wolves stayed far away because the dogs were awake.
In the village a teacher wrote letters on a slate.
Children copied the letters and learned to read simple words.
They read about cats and dogs and boats on a blue lake.
A fisherman cast a net and pulled in silver fish.
He cooked one fish and gave two to his neighbors.
The neighbors brought soup and sat by the fire to talk.
Talk turned to weather, harvest, and the coming winter.
Winter would bring snow and the need for wood and wool.
The woodcutter stacked logs beside the door before dusk.
His axe was sharp and his hands were strong from work.
A queen in a far story walked among her people with no crown.
She asked how the crops grew and listened to every answer.
A king mended a fence instead of sitting on a throne.
People said that was the best kind of king.
A dragon in a book guarded gold but wanted a friend.
A knight put down his sword and offered bread instead.
The dragon shared the gold and the village built a school.
The school had a bell that rang at noon and at dusk.
Students ran across the yard when the bell called them home.
Home was a cottage with a garden of beans and roses.
Bees moved from flower to flower and made sweet honey.
A girl tasted the honey and saved some for her brother.
Her brother drew a map of the river and the mill and the wood.
They followed the map and found a hidden pond of clear water.
Frogs sat on lily pads and jumped when a cloud covered the sun.
The cloud passed and the water shone like glass again.
A boat with a white sail drifted toward the harbor.
Sailors tied ropes and carried boxes of salt and cloth.
A merchant paid fairly and wrote the sums in a book.
The book kept the truth of each trade for later days.
Later days brought spring and green shoots in the fields.
Lambs ran beside their mothers on the new grass.
A poet wrote that hope is a seed that waits for rain.
Rain came and the seed became a tree that gave shade.
Under the shade a family ate bread and told the old stories.
The old stories taught care, patience, and courage.
Courage was a small act done when fear was near.
A child who feared the dark kept a candle by the bed.
The candle showed the chair, the door, and nothing more.
Nothing more was needed, and the child slept until dawn.
Dawn painted the hills pink and woke the roosters.
Roosters called and the day began with work and play.
Play was a game of stones and a race to the well.
Work was milking, sweeping, and mending a torn coat.
The torn coat became whole with needle, thread, and time.
Time moved like the river, never quite the same twice.
Yet each evening the same stars returned to the same sky.
People looked up and felt small and also not alone.
Not alone was the gift of neighbors, dogs, and songs.
Songs filled the hall on the feast of the harvest.
They sang of the mill, the miller, the grain, and the bread.
They sang of the girl who walked to the wood at sunrise.
They sang of the fox who learned to wait his turn.
And when the last song ended, they put out the lamps.
They went home under the moon and closed their doors with care.
Tomorrow they would wake, work, share, and tell the story again.
Once upon a time was how every good story liked to begin.
The end of one day was the start of another, as it always is.
